## What's new

- **Five levels instead of three.** Every college on your list is now High reach, Reach, Target, Likely, or Safe. A
  student with top grades and scores sees the most selective colleges as a Reach, and a student further from their
  numbers sees them as a High reach, so the two no longer look the same. Still never a percentage.
- **A selectivity tier for every college.** One word for how hard a college is to get into: Ultra selective, Highly
  selective, Very selective, Selective, Accessible, or Open. It's read from what the college publishes (its admit rate
  for students from your state, its admitted students' test scores, how many admitted students enroll, and how many of
  its first-years return and graduate), and the ⓘ beside it lists those facts with their sources. Filter and sort by
  it on Explore, and compare it side by side on Compare.
- **Who you're compared with.** Instead of "your pool", the estimate now says it plainly: "applicants like you, from
  Indiana, were admitted at 71%". A new panel lists every rate the college publishes for your situation: your state,
  other states, everyone, your major, and automatic admission where a state program offers it.
- **Your plan orders itself.** Your Dream college comes first, then each level's colleges closest to the next level up.
  The planner no longer suggests early decision for a Dream that's a High reach, and it points out where applying
  early would help most.
- **Beyond grades and scores.** Add where you stand in your class, up to eight activities with the years you did them
  and your role each year, and awards from a reviewed list. Your courses now read as tracks by subject against your
  intended major. Each of these counts only at colleges that say they weigh it. You can also say whether you're the
  first in your family to go to college; the site shows it, but it doesn't change your level.
- **Legacy and recruiting, per college.** In the planner, mark a family connection or where you are in a coach's
  recruiting at a college on your list. In states that ban legacy preference, the site says so and names the law.

## Behind the scenes

How Quad computes a level is its own and stays private; the site says which kinds of information it uses and never
the weights. The estimate never uses race, ethnicity, sex, or religion, and outcomes students choose to share are
anonymized. Parents see everything their student enters.
