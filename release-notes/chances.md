## What's new

- **How each college reads a record.** A few plain sentences at the top of each college's admissions page say what it
  weighs most, how many of its first-years had a 3.75 or higher (when most do, a high GPA rarely sets anyone apart),
  how many sent test scores, how it uses class rank, which courses it expects, and, where the college says so, how
  it reads your intended major. Compare shows the same side by side.
- **Your courses, against your school.** List your AP, IB, dual-enrollment, and honors courses with their grades,
  taken and planned, on your profile or in your plan. The site reads them the way a counselor does: against what your
  own high school offers. College pages show how your courses line up with what each college recommends.
- **The admit rate for your pool.** Where a college publishes it, you see the rate for applicants like you: from your
  state, for your major, or guaranteed by a state program (Texas top 10%, UT Austin's top 5%, the UC system's local
  guarantee, Wisconsin, Arizona, Idaho).
- **Quad's estimate.** Reach, Target, or Likely for each college, from your numbers, your courses, your pool, and the
  college's own filings, with a "What went into this estimate" panel. It's on college pages, in your plan, and in
  Compare. Never a percentage.
- **Next year's courses.** When it's time to pick classes, the plan suggests up to two advanced courses your school
  offers, each with its reason, and says plainly when strong grades matter more than adding another. AP exam sign-up,
  exam, and score dates are on the calendar.
- **How well it did.** If you choose to share your results, they help check the estimate; the Data page will publish
  how well it did each season.

## Behind the scenes

The estimate runs only on Quad's servers, and the site shows what it used, never how the pieces are combined. The
admission-program and major-admission details were gathered from colleges' and states' published statements and will
be re-checked against each live page before launch. The planner's GPA estimate was retuned for the larger set of
colleges that now publish GPA data, so about 900 colleges have one again.
