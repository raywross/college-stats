## What's new

**The plan opens to Next steps.** Where it used to show a list and a calendar, the plan now opens to what to do this
season: three to six moves, each with a reason and one button. The season comes from the graduation year, so a
sophomore in October sees the same things whether they signed up in August or yesterday, and nobody is shown as
"behind" for things from before they joined. Parents see their own part first (the money conversation, the net price
calculators, the aid forms, the cost check before a binding round) and the student's moves beside it.

**Good ED opportunities, flagged by name.** On at most three colleges on the list, from junior spring, a pill says
*Good ED opportunity* (or *Good ED II opportunity*): the college publishes early-decision counts and Quad's estimate
says a binding early application would move it up a level for this student. Tapping shows the college's own numbers,
the caveat about recruited athletes and legacies, three questions to answer for yourself, and a button that sets the
round. Quad never says "apply"; the student decides.

**Interest where it counts.** Where a college reports that applicant interest matters and the student stands close
enough for it to count, a move suggests one act of interest in the order the evidence supports: a visit, then
contact or an information session, then a follow. Every ED candidate gets a visit move before the decision.

**The shape of a good list.** Seniors aim for 12 to 15 colleges: up to two High reaches, two or three Reaches, five to
eight Targets, two or three Likelies, two or three Safes, and at least one Likely or Safe the family can afford.
Juniors build a longer list to visit and cut. The plan names one gap at a time and offers colleges at the missing
level with one-tap Add.

**Seniors: submit to deposit.** Each college gets its cards in turn: submit and mark applied, confirm the portal has
everything, record the decision, ED II after a deferral, compare the offers, decide by May 1, and, once, tell Quad
where you landed (opt in; it helps the next class's estimate).

**Your story.** Three private prompts in junior year, in the student's own words, read again when the essay window
opens. Nobody else sees them, and nobody but the student writes a word.

**Briefs that bring you back.** One email when a season turns, for the student and separately for each parent; the
week's moves in the Sunday mail that already goes out; a text the day before a hard date; and a dot on Plan when
something new is waiting. Nothing sends instantly, and email stays off until the site's sending is set up.

## Behind the scenes

A season function anchored on the graduation year replaces the planner's grade boundaries. Moves are the planner's
existing task rows with a season and an action, generated by the same idempotent generators, so the calendar, the
feed, the weekly mail, and the parent summary read them without new plumbing. The three rule sets (ED opportunities,
interest, list shape) are pure modules over the estimate's level, closeness, and ED verdict; nothing from the estimate
but the level ever reaches a card or pill. Built with the `build-roadmap-section` skill: nine units on sub-branches
(`specs/planner/next-steps/build-plan.md`).
