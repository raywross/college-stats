## What's new

The Essays tab shows each college's prompts as the college publishes them. About a fifth of Common App colleges never
publish them: the questions exist only inside the application form. The University of Florida is one; its own site
says only that the UF Supplement "includes a few additional short-answer questions" and sends applicants to the form.

This plan fills that gap without pretending. A prompt on the site will carry one of three labels:

- **As the college publishes it**: quoted from the college's own page, as today.
- **From the application form, confirmed**: read inside the form by a person each August, or reported by two or more
  families who met the same question while filling in their own application and chose to share it; the owner approves
  each one before it shows.
- **Likely this year's prompt**: listed by College Essay Guy, the best-kept public record of these questions, shown
  with their credit and a link once they have agreed in writing, and only where the college publishes nothing.

Sharing a question is a single unchecked box when a student adds a prompt of their own; it sends the question, never
the draft or a name.

## Behind the scenes

The college's own page always wins, and its "no supplement" is never overridden by a secondhand source. Nothing
automated ever signs in to an application platform. The reader's own misses, colleges that do publish prompts one link
past their how-to-apply page, are being fixed separately.
